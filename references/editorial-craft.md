# Editorial craft

Use this reference for creative and technical editing decisions. Start from the viewer's changing knowledge and emotion, then choose the least conspicuous technique that produces the intended change.

## Cut decision

Before every cut, identify:

1. what the viewer should feel;
2. what new information they need;
3. the section's energy and breathing room;
4. how the outgoing audio or movement motivates the next image.

Default to a hard cut. Use a J-cut when incoming sound should create anticipation, an L-cut when the current thought should continue over new evidence, a cutaway to clarify or hide a compression, a match cut to connect shape/motion/idea, and a smash cut only for intentional contrast. Cut on action or at a natural speech boundary. Preserve deliberate pauses; remove silence or filler only when meaning, character, and comic timing survive.

Pacing must vary with content. Short shots create urgency, medium holds support explanation, and long holds create weight or relief. A periodic visual change is an attention diagnostic, not a timer: change state when the information or emotion changes.

## Transitions and time

- Use clean cuts for most transitions. A dissolve signals gentle time or emotional change; a fade marks closure; a wipe, zoom, whip, flash, or glitch needs a semantic reason.
- Keep energetic transitions brief and reserve them for chapter boundaries, impacts, location changes, or deliberate comedy.
- Ease speed ramps over a perceptible interval and anchor them to action. Do not change dialogue speed unless intelligibility and pitch remain acceptable.
- Use freeze frames, repetitions, or rapid montage only when they add emphasis, compression, or rhythm rather than conceal weak structure.

## Sound

Maintain this priority: dialogue, music, effects, ambience. Dialogue must stay intelligible on small speakers and headphones. For general web delivery, approximately -16 LUFS integrated and -1.5 dBTP is a practical starting point, not a universal mandate.

- Add 20–50 ms fades at edit boundaries to prevent clicks.
- Duck music beneath speech with transparent attack and release; restore it between phrases when the emotional curve benefits.
- Bridge visual cuts with room tone or continuing audio when discontinuity would be distracting.
- Place effects on visible causes or story beats. Layer impact, texture, and tail only when each layer contributes.
- Check sync and loudness near the beginning, middle, end, and at the loudest transition.

## Captions and kinetic type

- Preserve factual wording in the raw transcript. Correct display captions separately and flag uncertain names, figures, and acronyms.
- Use no more than two lines when possible. Break on syntax and meaning, not arbitrary character counts.
- Keep captions in a protected safe region and above every overlay. Check the smallest aspect-ratio variant and the longest line.
- Highlight the currently important word or phrase, not every token. Map type motion to the spoken intent: reveal for discovery, scale for importance, direction for causality, strike-through for correction, and counters for measurable change.
- Keep caption timing tied to word boundaries. Avoid animation that delays reading or competes with the speaker's face.

## Color

Correct before styling: normalize exposure and white balance, match shots within a scene, then add a restrained look. Protect skin tones and important brand colors. Compare a hero frame against each corrected shot, inspect scopes or signal statistics when available, and verify that render color tags match the pixels. Check shadows, gradients, and saturated graphics for clipping or banding.

## Motion and compositing

Build each animated beat with a primary action, subordinate response, and optional ambient layer. Entrance motion decelerates; exit motion accelerates. Heavy elements generally move longer and settle more slowly than light elements. Use anticipation, follow-through, arcs, and overshoot only when the emotional personality supports them.

For overlays, protect faces, captions, and key evidence. Synchronize overlay time to the start of its display window. Use picture-in-picture for continuity, split screens for true comparison, and generated imagery only when it cannot be mistaken for documentary evidence.

## Delivery check

Before release, verify narrative continuity, flash frames, audio clicks, caption timing, color consistency, aspect ratio, codec, pixel format, audio format, duration, and playback from beginning to end. Render a high-quality master first when practical, then derive platform variants from it without repeatedly compositing the same layers.
