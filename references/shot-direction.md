# Shot direction and cinematic product storytelling

Use for product promos, screen recordings, UI demonstrations, motion-heavy explainers, and any project that needs a deliberate shot language rather than generic overlays.

## Choose the collaboration mode

- **Autonomous:** the user delegates creative decisions; record the brief, design direction, shot map, and tradeoffs, then continue without repeated approval gates.
- **Co-created:** the user wants control; confirm the brief, visual direction, shot map, and storyboard before costly production.
- **Template-led:** the user names a template or reference; preserve its structural grammar while reskinning typography, color, material, pacing, and content to the current subject.

Do not force a mode question when the user's intent is already clear.

## Derive the visual language

For a real product or brand, first extract the visual tokens that are safe and relevant: typefaces, weights, hierarchy, spacing, grid, corner radii, background/surface/text/accent colors, density, imagery, and material treatment. Use real screenshots for claims about actual UI. Redact personal, customer, internal, credential, and live production data before capture.

A styleframe is a production decision, not decoration. It must show the dominant hierarchy, safe caption region, speaker/product balance, palette, material, and one representative motion state.

## Shot-function vocabulary

Build shots by function, then choose a treatment:

| Function | Useful treatments |
|---|---|
| establish | wide interface/world view, map, overview, calm camera settle |
| orient | labeled regions, cursor path, progressive reveal, spatial push |
| demonstrate | real state change, before/after, step sequence, screen crop |
| prove | source screenshot, measured result, comparison, annotated evidence |
| explain | diagram, causal flow, timeline, hierarchy, exploded layers |
| emphasize | controlled push-in, isolation, scale contrast, brief hold |
| transform | morph, assemble, stack, split, state-to-state transition |
| transition | shared motion, shape/color match, audio bridge, motivated wipe |
| breathe | face time, stable hero frame, ambient motion, room tone |

Each shot gets one hero action. Reserve a motion device as the hero once per sequence unless repetition itself communicates a system. Budget a still hold after important information lands.

## Shot specification

For each shot record: purpose, claim, source asset, framing, camera path, hero action, supporting motion, duration, entry/exit condition, caption region, sound cue, and validation frame. Use absolute source time for footage and composition frames for animation.

For beat-driven music, detect actual transients and establish BPM/phase before final timing. Put major impacts on verified accents, not merely a mathematical grid. Render a sample and measure cut-to-transient error. Keep global camera slams rare; most beat reactions should affect a local element.

## Verification

Render representative stills from the opening, peak action, information landing, and exit of every substantial shot. Compare against the brief/styleframe for hierarchy, product truth, legibility, data privacy, and visual continuity. Then review the full sequence for camera comfort, holds, audio punctuation, and repeated tricks.
